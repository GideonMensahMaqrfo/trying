Document checklist

Most international shipments need the documents below. We tell you exactly which ones apply when we quote.

For every shipment

  • Commercial invoice: seller, buyer, goods, value and currency
  • Packing list: number of pieces, weights and sizes
  • Transport document: bill of lading (sea), air waybill (air) or CMR (road)
  • EORI number for businesses importing to or exporting from the EU or UK

Sometimes needed

  • Certificate of origin, to claim lower duty rates
  • Import or export licence for controlled goods
  • Health or phytosanitary certificates for food, plants and timber
  • Dangerous goods declaration and safety data sheet (SDS)
  • Vehicle title and ID when shipping cars

Restricted and prohibited items

Some goods cannot be shipped at all; others can travel only with special packing, labels and paperwork. Always tell us exactly what you are sending.

We do not accept

  • Illegal drugs and narcotics
  • Weapons, ammunition and explosives
  • Counterfeit or pirated goods
  • Cash, bank notes and bearer documents
  • Live animals
  • Goods banned in the origin or destination country

Accepted with conditions

  • Lithium batteries and electronics containing them
  • Paints, perfumes, aerosols and other flammable items
  • Chemicals and other dangerous goods (UN-numbered)
  • Food, plants, seeds and timber packaging
  • Vehicles, engines and parts containing fuel or oil
  • Medicines and medical devices

Packing tips

Use strong outer packaging

Double-wall boxes for parcels; pallets or wooden crates for heavy goods. Fill empty space so nothing moves.

Palletise for safety

Stack boxes squarely on the pallet, heaviest at the bottom, with no overhang. Strap and stretch-wrap the load.

Label every piece

Show sender, receiver and tracking number on each piece, and number them “1 of 4”, “2 of 4” and so on.

Use treated wood

Wooden pallets and crates for export must meet the ISPM 15 standard and carry its heat-treatment mark.
